## Why This Exists

Augustine's second warfare vocabulary is **inward**. In *City of God* he wrote that two loves built two cities: *amor sui usque ad contemptum Dei* — love of self to the contempt of God — built the earthly city; and *amor Dei usque ad contemptum sui* — love of God to the contempt of self — built the heavenly one. Every person holds both cities inside them, and the real war is between those two loves.

This program trains the child to fight that war well: to know what they love and in what order, to detect when a love is disordered, to recognize the Accuser's oldest tactics operating on them, and to use the sacramental defenses — starting with themselves.

## Relationship to `docs/07` — Extend, Do Not Duplicate

`docs/07_spiritual_warfare_discernment.md` already supplies the machinery this program **reuses as a given**:

- The **Accuser's mechanism**: projection and transference — he takes his own disorder and projects it outward, trying to make others believe they are as broken as he is.
- The **conviction-vs-accusation diagnostic**: God convicts to heal (specific, leads to repair); the Accuser shames to destroy (vague, leads to paralysis).
- The **sacramental ladder**: individual → marriage → family → community → nation → humanity, each with its own attack and defense.

This document adds the specifically **Augustinian** layer `docs/07` does not develop:

- **The two cities / two loves** (*City of God*, Bk XIV): citizenship is decided by *what* you love and *in what order*.
- **Ordo amoris — rightly ordered love.** Sin is usually not loving evil things; it is loving real goods in the wrong **order** or **amount** — a lesser good above a greater, or anything above God. This is the diagnostic engine stacked on top of the conviction/accusation test.
- **The restless heart** (*Confessions* I.1): "You have made us for Yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in You." Disordered love is the root of the interior war; restlessness is its symptom.

So: `docs/07` answers "who is attacking and whose voice is this?" This program answers "what are my loves, are they in order, and how are they re-ordered?"

## Ordo Amoris — The Core Diagnostic

Most of what goes wrong in a human life is not the love of something evil. It is a **good thing loved out of order**:

- A real good (a relationship, a skill, approval, comfort, safety, even a virtue) loved **more than God**, or **more than a greater good**.
- The disorder is in the **rank** or the **amount**, not in the object itself.

This reframes the moral life from "stop wanting bad things" to "get your loves in the right order." It also connects directly to the Ten Commandments (the 1st commandment is the original ordo amoris: nothing above God) and to the operational-security theme elsewhere in the system — a disordered love is exactly the leash another person can grab.

## The Accuser, Turned Inward

`docs/07` teaches the Accuser's tactics. This program's hardest move is to apply them **to oneself**:

- **Catch the Accuser**: when self-accusation or despair arrives, run the diagnostic — conviction or accusation? whose problem is being projected? toward communion or isolation? away from the sacraments?
- **Am I the Accuser?**: the harder half. Identify a time *you* took your own unreconciled wound and projected it onto someone else, making them the enemy — then repair it. The Accuser's signature success is getting *you* to accuse. A child who only ever diagnoses others has missed the point entirely.

## Guardrails

- This is **not** demon-spotting behind every annoying person. The Accuser's move is to get *you* to accuse; the program therefore requires honest self-implication, never only diagnosing others.
- Do **not** reward performed piety ("I love God most") offered with no real self-examination.
- Reject invented or hypothetical interior moments where a real one is required.
- Echo `docs/13`: examined faith over checkbox holiness; honor without blindness; faith without coercion.
- Tag these practices `catholic_formation` as well as `spiritual` where they reuse Reconciliation and examination of conscience, so they count in both pillars.
- The whole Augustinian point: the sword saves no soul, and the only enemy you are guaranteed to meet is the disorder in your own heart.
