# Research: Alternating Legs When Climbing and Jumping

Date: 2026-11-21. Purpose: establish the evidence-based age windows for
alternating-leg climbing and jumping, then add milestones to the Early Growth
Markers corpus. APPROVED by the owner and APPLIED to the corpus (see section 5).

## Sources (public-domain facts only)

- CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." movement milestones (2-60 months).
- Gross-motor development sequences published in the public literature from
  standardized instruments (Bayley-4, Peabody PDMS-2 norm summaries) and
  physical-education benchmarks (AAHPERD / PE Central), as summarized in
  publicly available developmental references. We summarize the consensus
  windows in our own words; no proprietary item text is reproduced.

## 1. The stair-climbing progression (alternating feet)

The universally described sequence:

| Age window | Skill |
|---|---|
| ~24-30 mo | Walks UP stairs holding rail/hand, BOTH feet per step ("marking time") |
| ~30-36 mo | Up with rail, beginning to alternate feet on some steps |
| ~36-42 mo | Walks UP stairs ALTERNATING feet (with rail or hand support) |
| ~42-48 mo | Walks DOWN stairs ALTERNATING feet (with rail; descending is harder: balance + depth perception) |
| ~48-60 mo | Up AND down alternating feet without support |

Key point: descending comes LATER than ascending (roughly 6-12 months later)
because it requires controlled eccentric strength and depth perception.

## 2. Playground ladder / rung climbing

Alternating hands-and-feet on vertical rungs (playground ladder, climbing
wall) is the same alternating pattern under load: consensus window
~4-5 years for a standard playground ladder, with beginner-friendly rungs
from ~3.5-4 years. Requires grip strength + bilateral coordination.

## 3. The jumping progression, and where "alternating legs" fits

| Age window | Skill |
|---|---|
| ~24 mo | Jumps in place with both feet leaving the ground |
| ~28-30 mo | Jumps forward with both feet |
| ~30-36 mo | Jumps off a low step with both feet |
| ~36 mo | Jumps over a low object/line |
| ~42-48 mo | Hops 1-3 times on ONE foot |
| ~48-54 mo | Gallops (asymmetric alternating: lead leg + trailing leg) |
| ~5-6 yr | Skips (alternating legs + rhythm) and JUMPING JACKS / scissor jumps (simultaneous bilateral alternating arms + legs) |

Key point: "alternating legs WHILE jumping" (scissor jumps, jumping jacks)
is a bilateral-coordination milestone. Before ~5 years children jump with
both legs TOGETHER; true alternating-leg airborne actions require rhythm,
midline crossing, and independent leg control, which consolidate at
**5-6 years**. This is later than alternating feet on stairs, because the
child must generate the alternation themselves in the air (stairs provide
the step edges as external cues; jumping jacks do not).

## 4. What our corpus already covers (Early Growth Markers, gross motor)

| Age group | Current gross-motor items | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| 24mo | Walks up/down a few stairs holding on; (30mo) jumps with both feet; climbs on/off furniture | Matches the early sequence |
| 36mo | Climbs well and runs easily; **Walks up stairs with one foot per step**; pedals a tricycle | UP-alternating is ALREADY covered at 36mo — matches the 36-42mo window |
| 48mo | Hops and stands on one foot for a few seconds; catches a bounced ball | Missing: DOWN-alternating stairs; ladder/rung climbing |
| 60mo | Hops on one foot and may skip; somersault/swings/climbs | Missing: jumping jacks / scissor jumps (alternating legs while jumping) |

## 5. Corpus additions (APPROVED by owner 2026-11-21 — applied to the corpus)

1. **48mo gross motor**: "Walks DOWN stairs alternating feet, holding the rail
   at first." (descending is the later half of the stair progression)
2. **48mo gross motor**: "Climbs a playground ladder or climbing wall,
   alternating feet and hands." (the same alternating pattern under load)
3. **60mo gross motor**: "Does jumping jacks or scissor jumps: jumps while
   alternating legs (and arms) in rhythm." (the bilateral-coordination
   capstone of the jumping progression)

Each addition sits exactly where the research places it, and each extends an
existing progression rather than starting a new one.
