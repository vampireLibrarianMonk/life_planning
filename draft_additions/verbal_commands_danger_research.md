# Research: Following Verbal Commands and Sense of Danger

Date: 2026-11-21. Purpose: establish the evidence-based progression for (1)
listening to and acting on verbal commands, and (2) danger awareness, before
adding milestones to the Early Growth Markers corpus. FOR OWNER REVIEW — no
corpus changes yet.

## Sources (public-domain facts only)

- CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." language and cognitive milestones.
- Receptive-language and executive-function sequences published in the public
  literature (Bayley-4 / PDMS-2 norm summaries; working-memory development).
- Child-safety research on risk perception and the teaching method that works
  (concrete rules + supervised practice + why-explanations), as summarized in
  publicly available safety-education references (AAP, Safe Kids, traffic
  research on children's road-crossing competence).

## 1. Following verbal commands (receptive language + working memory)

| Age window | Skill |
|---|---|
| ~12 mo | Follows a simple request PAIRED WITH A GESTURE ("come here" + reach) |
| ~18 mo | Follows a 1-step direction WITHOUT a gesture ("sit down") |
| ~24 mo | Follows 2-step instructions ("pick up the ball and put it in the box") |
| ~36 mo | Follows 2-3 step instructions in a row ("take off your shoes and put them in the closet") |
| ~4-5 yr | Follows multi-step sequenced directions; begins carrying out instructions after a delay ("when we get home, hang up your coat") |
| ~5-7 yr | Follows complex multi-step instructions with conditionals ("after you hang your coat, wash your hands, then come to the table"); working memory supports delayed execution |

Key point: the progression is driven by WORKING MEMORY growth, not obedience.
Each step up roughly doubles the memory load (1 item -> 2 items -> sequence ->
sequence + delay). A child who "doesn't listen" at 3 is often a child who
cannot yet HOLD a 3-step chain, not a child refusing.

## 2. Sense of danger (risk awareness + safety rules)

| Age window | Skill |
|---|---|
| ~18-24 mo | Learns IMMEDIATE physical dangers by rote + constant supervision: hot, sharp, the street. Rules are memorized, not understood |
| ~24-36 mo | Stops at the curb when told; names the danger objects when asked ("is the stove hot?"); still cannot apply rules alone for more than a moment |
| ~3-4 yr | Can STATE the family danger rules and answer "what happens if..."; applies them with reminders; water + street still require arm's-length supervision |
| ~4-5 yr | Begins to anticipate consequences ("if I run into the street, a car might hit me"); applies familiar rules with fewer reminders in FAMILIAR places |
| ~5-7 yr | Applies danger rules without reminders in familiar settings; can explain WHY each rule exists; still unreliable in NOVEL environments (impulse + novelty override knowledge) |
| ~7-10 yr | Reliable in familiar settings; beginning to assess unfamiliar situations; complex road-crossing competence develops gradually (traffic research places independent road-crossing at ~10-11 years) |
| ~10-12 yr | Independent risk assessment in new environments begins |

Key points:
- "Sense of danger" is BUILT, not innate: it is concrete rules + supervised
  practice + why-explanations, repeated in real settings. Vague warnings
  ("be careful") teach nothing; specific rules ("stop at the curb") do.
- The real risk ranking for young children is TRAFFIC and WATER, not
  strangers. Stranger-safety teaching matters but the daily-risk items are
  hot/sharp/street/water.
- Supervision requirements shrink as rules become automatic — the milestone
  is "applies without reminders in familiar places."

## 4. PROPOSED corpus additions (pending owner approval — not yet applied)

**Verbal commands (communication domain):**
1. **36mo**: "Follows instructions with two or three steps in a row (take off
   your shoes and put them in the closet)."
2. **48mo**: "Carries out a multi-step instruction after a delay (when we get
   home, hang up your coat)."
3. **60mo**: "Follows complex multi-step instructions with conditionals (after
   you hang your coat, wash your hands, then come to the table)."

**Sense of danger (thinking domain — anticipating consequences):**
4. **24mo**: "Stops at the curb when told (holds an adult's hand near the
   street)."
5. **36mo**: "Names the family danger rules when asked: hot things, sharp
   things, the street, water, and going with unknown adults."
6. **48mo**: "Explains why each family danger rule exists (what could happen
   if it is broken)."
7. **60mo**: "Applies the family danger rules without reminders in familiar
   places (kitchen, street curb, pool)."

Each sits exactly where the research places it and extends the existing
instruction-following progression (12mo gesture -> 18mo 1-step -> 24mo 2-step
-> 36mo 2-3 step -> 48mo delayed -> 60mo conditional). The danger items form
a NEW progression that pairs with the existing body-safety and secrets items.
